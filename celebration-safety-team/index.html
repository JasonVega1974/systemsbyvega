EMERGENCY PROCEDURES
Quick-reference response protocols. Based on published guidance from CISA, FEMA, Ready.gov, and NCMEC — adapt details to this facility with your Team Lead.
🚑 In any life-threatening emergency, call 911 first. Nampa Police non-emergency: (208) 465-2257 · Church office: (208) 466-5433
🔫Active Shooter — Run · Hide · Fight▶

Federal guidance (DHS/CISA/FBI) gives three options to consider, listed here in the order they are generally preferred. CISA deliberately frames these as options rather than a fixed 1-2-3 sequence: you take the one the situation actually allows, and that decision belongs to whoever is in the room.

  1. RUN — if there is an accessible escape path, evacuate. Leave belongings, help others if possible, keep hands visible, and get people moving away from the threat.
  2. HIDE — if evacuation is not possible, get out of the shooter's view. Lock/blockade doors, silence phones, stay quiet and behind solid objects.
  3. FIGHT — as a last resort and only when your life is in imminent danger, act with physical aggression and improvised weapons to incapacitate the shooter.

When law enforcement arrives: keep hands visible and empty, follow all commands immediately, avoid pointing or yelling, and do not stop officers to ask for help — they will move toward the threat first.

Call 911 when safe. Give location, number/description of shooter(s), weapons, and number of potential victims.

📚 Team standard: complete the Academy module "Active Shooter Response," review the CISA Active Shooter Preparedness resources, and take free FEMA course IS-907.A: Active Shooter — What You Can Do.
🧒Lost Child — Code Adam▶

Code Adam is the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children's free missing-child protocol. NCMEC reports it is in use in hundreds of thousands of locations nationwide. NCMEC's six steps, in order:

  1. Get a description. Name, age, gender, hair and eye colour, height, weight, and what the child is wearing — including shoes (clothing can be changed quickly; shoes usually aren't). Get a photo if the guardian has one on their phone.
  2. Page "Code Adam" with the description over the team channel to all team members and staff.
  3. Begin the search. Assigned team members cover every exterior door and the parking lot — no child leaves unaccompanied — while others sweep the building: sanctuary, kids' rooms, restrooms, hallways. Escort the parent or guardian to the main entrance so they can help identify the child on sight.
  4. Call 911 immediately. Do not wait, and do not finish searching first. The search and the call happen at the same time.
  5. Locate the child. If found safe and unharmed, reunite with the verified parent/guardian and check the claim tag. If found with someone other than a parent or guardian, use reasonable, non-violent efforts to delay their departure and note description, vehicle, and direction of travel for police.
  6. Conclude the incident. Announce "Code Adam cancelled" so the building stands down, then report the incident to church leadership — including false alarms, which are the cheapest training data the team will ever get.
⏱️ There is no waiting period. NCMEC's instruction is "DON'T HESITATE, CALL 911 IMMEDIATELY." Any rule of thumb that sounds like "search for ten minutes first" is not part of Code Adam and must not be used here.
📚 Free official Code Adam kit, poster, training video and employee quiz: missingkids.org — Code Adam resources · program overview. Children's check-in tag matching is the everyday version of this protection — enforce it at every pickup.
🔥Fire & Evacuation▶
  1. On alarm or visible fire/smoke: activate the alarm (if not already), begin evacuation immediately, and call 911.
  2. Team members move to assigned zones: sanctuary doors, foyer, children's auditorium, and children's check-in. Children's workers evacuate kids by class; parents reunite at the assembly area, not in hallways.
  3. Direct people to the nearest safe exit — never through smoke. Assist those with mobility needs; know accessible routes in advance.
  4. Proceed to the designated assembly area (to be marked on the site schematic) and keep driveways clear for fire apparatus.
  5. Account for people; report anyone missing (and last known location) to arriving responders. Do not re-enter until fire officials clear the building.
📚 USFA — Workplace & non-residential fires · Ready.gov — Emergency response plans for organisations · U.S. Fire Administration. (v1 cited Ready.gov's home-fire page here. That page is written for a household — it has nothing to say about evacuating several hundred people from an assembly occupancy with a children's wing.) Assembly-area location and route arrows must be added to the uploaded site schematic.
🌪️Severe Weather — Tornado / Inclement Weather▶
  1. Monitor NWS alerts (the Boise office covers Nampa). A Watch = conditions are possible, be ready; a Warning = it is happening or imminent, act now.
  2. Tornado warning: move everyone to interior rooms and hallways on the lowest level, away from windows. Crouch low, cover heads.
  3. The sanctuary is not a shelter. The National Weather Service is explicit: do not go to large open rooms such as cafeterias, gymnasiums or auditoriums — a wide-span roof is the first thing to fail. That rules out the sanctuary and the fellowship hall, which is exactly where a congregation's instinct is to stay put. Move them.
  4. Do not shelter in vehicles. (If someone is already driving, NWS guidance is to drive to the nearest sturdy shelter rather than stay in the car.)
  5. For winter storms/ice: leadership decides on delays/cancellations early; team assists with parking-lot conditions, entrance safety, and communication.
📚 Ready.gov — Tornadoes · NWS — Tornado Safety (the large-room rule) · NWS Boise · Ready.gov — Winter Weather
🌍Earthquake — Drop · Cover · Hold On▶
  1. DROP to hands and knees, COVER head and neck (under sturdy furniture if possible), HOLD ON until shaking stops.
  2. Do not run outside during shaking; most injuries come from falling objects and glass. Stay away from windows and tall shelving.
  3. After shaking stops: check for injuries and hazards (gas smell, structural damage, fire), then evacuate calmly to the assembly area if the building is unsafe. Expect aftershocks.
📚 Ready.gov — Earthquakes · ShakeOut — Drop, Cover, Hold On. Why this matters here: Idaho's damaging earthquakes have been in the central part of the state — Borah Peak (1983, M6.9) and near Stanley (2020, M6.5), both roughly 250–300 km from Nampa. Nampa sits in the western Snake River Plain, not on those faults. The realistic local exposure is strong felt shaking from a distant event during a service, which is enough to cause panic, falls and falling fixtures in a full sanctuary.
🌊Flooding▶
  1. Move people away from flooded areas and to higher floors/ground as needed; shut off electricity to affected areas if safe to do so.
  2. Turn Around, Don't Drown — never direct traffic or people through flooded sections of the parking lot or roadway. NWS figures: 6 inches of fast-moving water knocks an adult over · 12 inches carries away most cars · 2 feet carries away SUVs and trucks. A church van is not an exception to this.
  3. Report facility flooding to leadership and, if hazardous, call 911 / utilities.
📚 Ready.gov — Floods · NWS Flood Safety
🕊️De-escalation — Disruptive or Agitated Individuals▶

Most incidents at churches are resolved with calm words, not force. Core principles taught by the Crisis Prevention Institute (CPI), Verbal Judo, and church-security trainers:

  • Be empathic and listen actively — let the person vent; acknowledge feelings without agreeing to demands ("I can hear that you're upset — let's figure this out").
  • Respect personal space — CPI says stand at least 1.5 to 3 feet away from someone who is escalating; crowding raises anxiety. Keep non-threatening body language: hands visible, relaxed posture, calm and low tone of voice.
  • Ask open-ended questions and offer choices ("Would you rather step outside to talk, or sit down over here?") — choices restore a sense of control.
  • Set clear, simple limits and allow silence and time for decisions. Avoid commands like "calm down," arguing, or matching their volume.
  • Work in pairs; one talks, one observes. Position between the person and the congregation with an exit route for both of you.
  • If de-escalation fails or a weapon/threat appears, disengage to protect people and call 911. Safety over resolution — every time.
📚 CPI — Top 10 De-escalation Tips (free) · Verbal Judo — church security de-escalation
🏛️Church Continuity of Operations▶

Continuity planning keeps ministry running after an emergency (fire, flood, violence, loss of facility). Following FEMA's continuity guidance, the church plan should identify:

  • Essential functions — worship services, children's ministry, communications, giving/finance, pastoral care.
  • Orders of succession & delegations — who leads if the Senior Pastor or a key staff member is unavailable.
  • Alternate facilities — pre-identified worship/meeting locations and an online service option.
  • Communications — how staff, team, and congregation are notified (call tree, text, email, social).
  • Vital records — insurance policies, member data, and financial records backed up off-site.
📚 FEMA — Continuity Resources · Ready.gov — Continuity Planning · FEMA's Guide for Developing High-Quality EOPs for Houses of Worship
